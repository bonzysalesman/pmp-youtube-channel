This covers managing and leading teams, supporting performance, empowering stakeholders, and managing conflict. Think of this as the 'soft skills' of project management, though they're anything but soft - they're critical. The Process domain is the largest at 50% - about 90 questions. This covers planning, executing, closing, managing changes, optimizing performance, and integrating activities. This is your traditional project management processes. The Business Environment domain is 8% - about 14 questions. This covers compliance, benefits realization, organizational change, and knowledge transfer. This is about understanding the bigger picture - how projects fit into organizational strategy. Notice that Process and People together make up 92% of the exam. That's where we'll spend most of our time."

## Slide 8: Question Types Deep Dive (5 min)
**Visual**: Use Day_1_Slide_5.png (expanded with examples)

### On-Screen Text:
# Question Types You'll Encounter

## 1️⃣ Multiple Choice (Most Common)
- Select ONE best answer from 4 options
- Usually scenario-based
- **Strategy:** Eliminate obviously wrong answers first

## 2️⃣ Multiple Response
- Select ALL correct answers (typically 2-3)
- Question specifies how many to choose
- **Strategy:** Each option is true/false decision

## 3️⃣ Matching
- Connect items from two lists
- Common for processes, knowledge areas, tools
- **Strategy:** Start with ones you're certain about

## 4️⃣ Hotspot
- Click on specific areas of graphics/diagrams
- Often process flows or organizational charts
- **Strategy:** Understand the visual before clicking

## 5️⃣ Fill-in-the-Blank (Less Common)
- Type correct words or phrases
- Usually formulas or specific terminology
- **Strategy:** Spelling must be exact

### Speaker Notes (Verbatim):
"Let's dive deep into the five question types you'll encounter. Multiple choice is the most common format. You'll see a scenario - often a paragraph or two - followed by a question and four answer options. Your job is to select the ONE best answer. These test your ability to apply the PMP mindset to real situations. Strategy: eliminate obviously wrong answers first, then choose the best remaining option. Multiple response questions ask you to select ALL correct answers. The question will tell you how many - usually 2 or 3. Treat each option as a true/false decision. Matching questions have you connect items from two lists - like matching processes to knowledge areas, or tools to techniques. Start with the ones you're absolutely certain about, then work through the rest. Hotspot questions show you a diagram - maybe a process flow or organizational chart - and you click on the correct area. Make sure you understand the visual before clicking. Fill-in-the-blank questions are less common. You'll type specific terms or formulas. Spelling must be exact, so be careful. We'll practice all five types throughout this course."

## Slide 10: The PMP Mindset (5 min)
**Visual**: Create detailed mindset graphic

### On-Screen Text:
# The PMP® Mindset: Critical for Success

## Core Principles:

### 🎯 Proactive vs. Reactive
- Anticipate and prevent problems
- Don't just respond to issues
- **Example:** Establish team ground rules early vs. waiting for conflict

### 🤝 Servant Leadership
- Support and empower your team
- Remove obstacles and provide resources
- **Example:** "How can I help you succeed?" vs. "Why isn't this done?"

### 💎 Value-Driven Decisions
- Every choice creates business value
- Focus on outcomes, not just outputs
- **Example:** Recommend project changes if value is compromised

### 👥 Stakeholder-Centric
- Engage early and often
- Understand needs and expectations
- **Example:** Proactive communication vs. reactive reporting

### ⚖️ Ethical & Professional
- Responsibility, Respect, Fairness, Honesty
- Maintain confidentiality and integrity
- **Example:** Transparent communication even when difficult

### Speaker Notes (Verbatim):
"Now let's talk about something absolutely critical that many candidates overlook: the PMP Mindset. This isn't just about knowing processes - it's about thinking like a project management professional. Let me break down the core principles. First, proactive versus reactive thinking. A PMP doesn't wait for problems to occur - they anticipate and prevent them. For example, instead of waiting for team conflict to escalate, you establish team ground rules and conflict resolution processes during project initiation. Second, servant leadership. You're not there to command and control - you're there to support and empower your team. Ask 'How can I help you succeed?' instead of 'Why isn't this done?' Remove obstacles, provide resources, develop capabilities. Third, value-driven decisions. Every choice you make should create business value. Focus on outcomes and benefits, not just completing tasks. If a project's value is compromised, a PMP will recommend changes or even cancellation. Fourth, stakeholder-centric thinking. Engage stakeholders early and often. Understand their needs and expectations. Be proactive in communication, not just reactive reporting. Fifth, ethical and professional behavior. The four values are Responsibility, Respect, Fairness, and Honesty. Maintain confidentiality, act with integrity, communicate transparently even when it's difficult. This mindset is how you approach every exam question. When you see a scenario, think: what would a proactive, servant leader do to create value for stakeholders while acting ethically? That's your answer."
