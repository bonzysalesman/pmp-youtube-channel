# Strategic Reference: PMI Code of Ethics & Professional Conduct

## 🎯 The Examiner's Perspective
The PMI Code of Ethics is not just "good advice"—it is a foundational set of constraints that override project priorities like schedule and budget in situational questions. On the PMP exam, ethical dilemmas are common. Use this guide to align your judgment with the **Mandatory** and **Aspirational** standards of the profession.

---

## 🏛️ The Four Core Values

### 1. Responsibility
The duty to take ownership for the decisions we make (or fail to make) and the consequences that result.
- **Aspirational**: Making decisions based on the best interests of society, public safety, and the environment.
- **Mandatory**: 
    - Adhering to legal and regulatory requirements.
    - reporting unethical or illegal conduct.
    - Accepting only those assignments that are consistent with our background/skills.

### 2. Respect
The duty to show high regard for ourselves, others, and the resources entrusted to us.
- **Aspirational**: Listening to others' points of view and understanding them. Conduct ourselves in a professional manner even when it is not reciprocated.
- **Mandatory**:
    - Negotiating in good faith.
    - Not using our power to influence others' decisions for personal gain.
    - Respecting the property rights of others.

### 3. Fairness
The duty to make decisions impartially and objectively, free from competing self-interests, prejudice, and favoritism.
- **Aspirational**: Demonstrating transparency in decision-making and providing equal access to information.
- **Mandatory**:
    - Disclosing any real or potential conflict of interest.
    - Not discriminating based on gender, race, age, religion, etc.
    - Not awarding contracts to friends or family without impartial review.

### 4. Honesty
The duty to understand the truth and act in a truthful manner both in our communications and in our conduct.
- **Aspirational**: Earnestly seeking to understand the truth and providing accurate information in a timely manner.
- **Mandatory**:
    - Not engaging in or condoning deceptive behavior.
    - Providing accurate information about our professional qualifications.
    - Not using misleading information to influence stakeholders.

## ⚖️ Situational "Tie-Breaker" Rules
When faced with an ethical dilemma on the exam, remember:
1. **Compliance Over Schedule**: If an action is illegal or violates safety, the answer is *never* to do it, even if it saves the project.
2. **Duty to Report**: If you see a colleague violating the code (e.g., stealing project funds), your mandatory duty is to **Report** it to their manager or the appropriate authority.
3. **Conflict Disclosure**: The first step in any conflict of interest (e.g., your brother owns the bidding vendor) is to **Disclose** the relationship to the Sponsor or CCB. 
4. **Professionalism in Conflict**: Even when stakeholders are abusive or unprofessional, the PM must maintain **Respect** and professional conduct.
