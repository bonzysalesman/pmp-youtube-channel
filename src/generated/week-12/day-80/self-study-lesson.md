# Self-Study Lesson: Day 80 - The Finisher's Toolkit

## 🧠 The Strategic Perspective
Welcome to Day 80. You have completed the first full simulation. Today, we don't look at "What we know"—we look at "What we missed." This lesson is a curated review of the most high-impact concepts that frequently trip up candidates on their first attempt.

## 1. The EVM "Safe Zone" (Earned Value Management)
Don't memorize—understand the *Meaning*.
- **CPI/SPI > 1**: Good (Under Budget / Ahead of Schedule).
- **CPI/SPI < 1**: Bad (Over Budget / Behind Schedule).
- **CV/SV > 0**: Good.
- **CV/SV < 0**: Bad.
- **EAC (Estimate at Completion)**:
    - *Typical Performance*: `BAC / CPI`
    - *Atypical Performance*: `AC + (BAC - EV)`
    - *Strategic Tip*: If the question says "The original plan is flawed," use the atypical formula. If it says "Trends will continue," use the typical one.

---

## 2. The Conflict Resolution Hierarchy (The "Tuckman Plus")
When People clash, how does the Strategic Peer act?
1. **Collaborate / Problem Solve**: (Win-Win) - Always the #1 goal.
2. **Compromise / Reconcile**: (Lose-Lose partially) - Second best.
3. **Withdraw / Avoid**: Only if the issue is trivial or "not your fight."
4. **Force / Direct**: Only in emergencies or if legal/safety is at risk.
5. **Strategic Pivot**: Focus on the **Root Cause**, not the personalities.

---

## 3. The "Analyze First" Mantra
The single biggest cause of wrong answers in the Process domain is taking an action before doing an analysis.
- **Order of Operations**:
    1. **Identify**: "A new risk/issue has appeared."
    2. **Analyze**: "What is the impact? Update the register." (**DO THIS FIRST**)
    3. **Plan**: "What are our options?"
    4. **Act**: "Implement the response or submit a Change Request."
    5. **Communicate**: "Inform the stakeholder of the outcome."

---

## 🎯 PMP Mindset Tip: "The Servant Leader's Ego"
In the PMP world, you are a facilitator, not a hero. If a question gives you an option to "Let the team decide" or "Ask the team for a solution," it is almost always better than "The PM decides."
