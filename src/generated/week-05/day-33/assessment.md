# Work Group 2: Process Domain (Part 2) - Review Assessment

## 📝 Strategic Context
This assessment focuses on the second half of the **Process Domain** (Starting the Project). As a Strategic Peer, your goal is to analyze these scenarios through the lens of a **Professional Project Manager** who prioritizes framework alignment, risk stewardship, and institutional integrity.

---

## ❓ The Assessment

### Question 1: The Schedule Squeeze
Your project is three weeks behind schedule due to a delayed material shipment. The project has a firm "Go-Live" date, and the Sponsor has explicitly stated that no additional funds will be released for this fiscal year. 
**What is your most appropriate strategic action?**
A. Crash the schedule by authorizing mandatory overtime for the core team.
B. Fast-track the schedule by overlapping the Quality Audit phase with the final Assembly phase.
C. Submit a change request to the CCB to extend the project deadline.
D. Reduce the project scope by removing low-priority features to meet the deadline.

---

### Question 2: The Logic of Quality
A team member discovers a recurring defect in a manufacturing process. While the current deliverables meet the minimum acceptance criteria, the team member suggests that the defect point will eventually lead to systemic failure. 
**Which quality tool should the Project Manager use to identify the root cause of this defect?**
A. Control Chart
B. Pareto Diagram
C. Fishbone Diagram
D. Scatter Diagram

---

### Question 3: The Cost of Quality
You are presenting the project budget to the Steering Committee. A stakeholder asks why you have allocated $50,000 for "Team Training and Process Documentation," arguing that this money should be spent on additional software testers at the end of the project.
**How should you respond using the PMP® Mindset?**
A. Agree with the stakeholder and move the funds to Appraisal costs to ensure delivery.
B. Explain that training is a Prevention cost, which is significantly more cost-effective than finding defects during inspection.
C. Explain that as the PM, you have sole authority over the quality budget allocation.
D. Suggest a compromise where 50% is spent on training and 50% on additional testers.

---

### Question 4: The Integrative Pivot
A Change Request for a new feature has been approved by the Change Control Board (CCB). The PM has updated the Scope Baseline.
**In the context of Integration Management, what is the next step the PM must take?**
A. Direct the team to begin work on the new feature immediately.
B. Update the Schedule and Cost baselines to reflect the ripple effects of the new scope.
C. Notify the Sponsor that the project's business value has increased.
D. Document the change in the Lessons Learned register.

### Question 5: The Procurement Dilemma
You are managing a highly innovative R&D project where the final output is unclearly defined. You need to hire a specialized lab to conduct unpredictable experiments.
**Which contract type provides the most strategic alignment for this situation?**
A. Firm-Fixed-Price (FFP)
B. Fixed-Price-Incentive-Fee (FPIF)
C. Cost-Plus-Fixed-Fee (CPFF)
D. Time and Materials (T&M)

### Question 6: The Ethics of Sourcing
During a Bidder Conference for a new IT infrastructure contract, a potential vendor who is a former colleague asks you a private question during the coffee break about the weighted criteria for the selection process.
**How must you respond to maintain professional integrity?**
A. Answer the question discreetly to maintain the relationship.
B. Tell them you cannot answer privately and repeat the question and your answer to all attendees when the session resumes.
C. Report the vendor to the Project Sponsor and disqualify them immediately.
D. Direct the vendor to the RFP document and refuse to answer any further questions.

### Question 7: The Governance Threshold
Your project schedule indicates a 2-day delay on a non-critical activity. Your Governance Plan states that any "Significant Schedule Variance" must be escalated to the Steering Committee. The plan defines "Significant" as a delay of >5 days on the Critical Path.
**What is your next action?**
A. Escalate the 2-day delay to the Steering Committee immediately.
B. Manage the delay at the team level and look for opportunities to recover the time within the float.
C. Update the Governance Plan to change the definition of "Significant."
D. Issue a formal Warning Letter to the owner of the delayed activity.

### Question 8: The Closure Protocol
A project has been successfully completed, and the customer has formally accepted all deliverables. The project team is anxious to move to their next assignments.
**Which of the following must occur before the Project Manager can formally close the project?**
A. The final project celebration must be held.
B. All project procurements must be formally closed.
C. The team must be assigned to their new projects in the HR system.
D. The Sponsor must sign off on the Project Charter.

---

### Question 9: The Terminated Project
Midway through execution, the organization's priorities shift, and your project is terminated. The Sponsor tells you to "drop everything" and start a different project tomorrow.
**What is the correct professional response?**
A. Follow the Sponsor's instructions to ensure you are seen as a "team player."
B. Insist on finishing the current phase before moving to the new project.
C. Perform the formal project closure process, including archiving records and capturing lessons learned for the work completed.
D. Simply update the status of the project to "Closed" in the PMO dashboard and move on.

---

### Question 10: The Transition Strategy
You are handing over a new financial reporting system to the Accounting Department. The Accounting Manager expresses concern that her team does not know how to run the new reports.
**Which component of the Project Management Plan should have addressed this?**
A. Procurement Management Plan
B. Scope Management Plan
C. Resource Management Plan
D. Project/Phase Transition Plan

---

## 🗝️ Answer Key & Rationales

### 1. Answer: B
**Rationale**: Crashing (A) costs money, which is forbidden by the Sponsor. Fast-tracking (B) increases risk but doesn't necessarily increase cost. Change requests (C) and scope reduction (D) are "secondary" options after you've attempted to optimize the schedule model yourself.

### 2. Answer: C
**Rationale**: The Fishbone Diagram (C) is a root cause analysis tool. Control Charts (A) show stability, Pareto (B) shows frequency/priority, and Scatter (D) shows correlation.

### 3. Answer: B
**Rationale**: In the PMP® Mindset, Prevention (Conformance) is always preferred over Inspection (Appraisal/Non-conformance). It is more cost-effective to prevent defects than to find them.

### 4. Answer: B
**Rationale**: Integration is about managing the ripple effect. A scope change almost always impacts schedule and cost. You must update the entire Performance Measurement Baseline (PMB) before executing.

### 5. Answer: C
**Rationale**: When scope is unclear/R&D, Cost-Plus (C) is the standard. FFP (A) would be too risky for the seller, and T&M (D) is usually for smaller, time-bound efforts.

### 6. Answer: B
**Rationale**: Fairness and Transparency are mandatory. Any information shared with one bidder must be shared with all bidders in a public forum.

### 7. Answer: B
**Rationale**: Follow the Governance Plan. The delay is not "Significant" by the plan's definition and is not on the Critical Path. The PM should manage it without escalation.

### 8. Answer: B
**Rationale**: Procurement Closure must always precede Project Closure.

### 9. Answer: C
**Rationale**: All projects, whether successful or terminated, must go through the formal closure process to preserve organizational history and archive assets.

### 10. Answer: D
**Rationale**: The Transition Plan (D) specifically outlines how project deliverables are moved into a permanent, operational state, including training and support.
