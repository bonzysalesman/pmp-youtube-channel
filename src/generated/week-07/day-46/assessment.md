# WG3 (Part 2) Review Assessment: Doing the Work

## 📝 Performance Objectives
This assessment verifies your mastery of the **Execution Governance** domain within the PMP Mastery Framework. A strategic peer must be able to protect project baselines, resolve immediate obstacles, and preserve institutional knowledge under pressure.

---

### Question 1: The Materialized Threat
During a critical software deployment, a pre-identified risk trigger is activated: the primary hosting provider reports a 4-hour service outage. The project manager's **first** action should be:
A. Update the Risk Register to reflect the occurrence.
B. Implement the pre-planned workaround.
C. Open an entry in the Issue Log and assess the impact on the deployment schedule.
D. Escalate the outage to the Project Sponsor immediately.

---

### Question 2: The Executive Favor
A high-ranking stakeholder, who is also a mentor to the Project Manager, sends an email requesting a "minor tweak" to the user interface that they claim will take less than an hour of developer time. How should the Project Manager respond?
A. Instruct the developer to implement the change as a sign of stakeholder goodwill.
B. Ask the stakeholder to submit a formal Change Request and perform an impact analysis.
C. Add the request to the Product Backlog for the next project phase.
D. Decline the request, citing the rigid adherence to the Scope Baseline.

---

### Question 3: The Tacit Knowledge Gap
The Lead Architect for a complex engineering project is retiring in one month. The Project Manager is concerned that the architect’s unique experience with the local soil conditions will be lost. To ensure effective **knowledge transfer**, the PM should:
A. Ask the architect to write a comprehensive technical report on soil conditions.
B. Organize a series of peer-shadowing sessions and storytelling workshops.
C. Upload all past architectural drawings to the Document Management System (DMS).
D. Update the Lessons Learned Register with the architect's contact information.

### Question 4: The CCB Decision
The Change Control Board (CCB) has approved a change request that adds a new security module to the project. What is the Project Manager's **next** step?
A. Update the Project Management Plan and relevant baselines.
B. Inform the team and begin implementation immediately.
C. Notify the requester that the change has been approved.
D. Update the Change Log and verify the implementation date.

### Question 5: The Supplier Dispute
A third-party vendor has delivered a component that meets the technical specifications but does not align with the Project Manager's "interpretation" of a subjective quality standard. The vendor insists they have met the contract obligations. The Project Manager should:
A. Refuse payment until the vendor meets the subjective standard.
B. File a formal claim through the legal department.
C. Review the contract’s SOW and terms to determine the legal obligation.
D. Seek a negotiated settlement to resolve the disagreement constructively.

### Question 6: The Critical Path Issue
An issue has been logged regarding a delay in a predecessor task which is NOT on the critical path. However, the Project Manager’s impact analysis shows that this delay will use up all the available float. This issue should be prioritized as:
A. Low Priority, as it doesn't affect the project finish date yet.
B. High Priority, as the task is effectively now on the critical path.
C. Medium Priority, to be addressed after critical path risks are mitigated.
D. No Priority, as the team should focus only on baselined work.

### Question 7: Explicit Knowledge Stewardship
The Project Manager is auditing the project’s **Configuration Management** system. They find that three different versions of the "Technical Requirements Document" are being used by different team members. This is a failure in:
A. Version Control and Artifact Retrieval.
B. Stakeholder Communication.
C. Tacit Knowledge Transfer.
D. Quality Control Inspections.

### Question 8: The Agile Change Mindset
In a Scrum-based project, the Product Owner discovers that a competitor has released a feature that makes one of the current Sprint's items obsolete. The Product Owner should:
A. Request an immediate change via the CCB.
B. Cancel the current Sprint.
C. Reprioritize the Product Backlog for future iterations.
D. Instruct the team to stop work on that specific item and switch tasks.

---

### Question 9: Root Cause Rigor
The team has faced the same server connectivity issue three times in the last month. Each time, a "restart" (workaround) fixed it temporarily. To address this as a **Strategic Peer**, the PM should:
A. Update the Issue Log and assign a permanent owner to perform restarts.
B. Facilitate a Root Cause Analysis (RCA) session to find the systemic failure.
C. Increase the contingency reserve for IT maintenance.
D. Escalate the IT department's poor performance to senior management.

---

### Question 10: The Escalation threshold
A Project Manager identifies that a necessary change to the project scope will cost $50,000. The Project Manager’s approval limit is $10,000, and the Change Management Plan states all larger changes must go to the CCB. However, the Sponsor is on vacation and the CCB won't meet for two weeks. The PM should:
A. Approve the change to prevent a schedule delay.
B. Use the Management Reserve to cover the cost without formal approval.
C. Wait for the CCB meeting, documenting the impact of the delay in the Change Log.
D. Ask the Deputy Sponsor for an emergency authorization under the defined governance rules.

---

## 🗝️ Answer Key & Rationale

1.  **C**. **Assess then Act**. You must first log the issue and understand its impact before you can implement the workaround or escalate.
2.  **B**. **Process Integrity**. Even mentors must follow the Change Management Plan. Impact analysis is the mandatory first step for any change.
3.  **B**. **Tacit Knowledge**. Reports (A) and DMS (C) are for *explicit* knowledge. *Tacit* knowledge requires human interaction, like shadowing and storytelling.
4.  **A**. **Maintenance of the Plan**. Once approved, the project "Bible" (the Plan) must be updated before implementation or communication can be considered complete.
5.  **D**. **Negotiation First**. The PMP mindset always prioritizes negotiated settlements over adversarial claims or legal action.
6.  **B**. **Schedule Logic**. Once float is consumed, the path becomes critical. This is a high-priority issue because any further delay impacts the project finish date.
7.  **A**. **Artifact Governance**. Configuration management is specifically about ensuring the integrity and correct versioning of project artifacts.
8.  **C**. **Agile Flexibility**. In Agile, the Backlog is the primary tool for managing change. The PO prioritizes for future value rather than disrupting the current sprint (unless the whole goal is defunct).
9.  **B**. **Eradicate the Cause**. A Strategic Peer moves from "fixing symptoms" (workarounds) to "eradicating causes" via RCA.
10. **D**. **Governance Stewardship**. You must follow the rules, but you must also be proactive. If the primary authority is absent, follow the secondary governance path defined in your plan.
