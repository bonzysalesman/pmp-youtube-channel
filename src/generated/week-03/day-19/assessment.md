# WG1 Comprehensive Practice Test: The People Domain

## 🏁 Test Instructions
- **Time Limit**: 52 Minutes (77 seconds/question).
- **Format**: 40 Multiple Choice Questions.
- **Protocol**: Choose the **BEST** answer for each scenario. Focus on proactive leadership, root cause identification, and servant leadership principles.

---

### Questions

**Question 1**
A key stakeholder frequently bypasses the project manager to give direct instructions to the development team, leading to scope creep and team frustration. What should the project manager do FIRST?
A. Inform the stakeholder that they must follow the formal communication plan.
B. Meet with the stakeholder to understand their concerns and discuss the impact on the project.
C. Escalate the stakeholder's behavior to the project sponsor for disciplinary action.
D. Tell the team to ignore any instructions not coming through the formal change control system.

**Question 2**
During a sprint retrospective, two senior developers argue over the technical approach for the next iteration. The argument becomes personal, and the team’s morale is visibly dropping. How should the project manager handle this conflict?
A. Step in and choose the technical approach that aligns with the project charter to end the debate.
B. Allow the team to continue the discussion until they reach a consensus, as they are self-organizing.
C. Facilitate a private meeting with the two developers to resolve the personal issues using emotional intelligence.
D. Ask the team to vote on the approach during the next daily stand-up.

**Question 3**
A project manager is leading a global virtual team. During a video call, they notice that team members from one specific region are not participating in discussions and appear hesitant to share feedback. What is the BEST action for the project manager?
A. Send a follow-up email requiring all team members to submit their feedback in writing.
B. Schedule individual one-on-one meetings with the quiet team members to build trust and understand their perspective.
C. Publicly ask the quiet team members for their opinion during the call to ensure their voices are heard.
D. Research the cultural norms of the region and adjust the team's communication protocols accordingly.

**Question 4**
A project team is consistently missing deadlines. The project manager conducts a root cause analysis and finds that the team lacks the specific technical skills required for the new software platform. What should the project manager do?
A. Replace the underperforming team members with more experienced contractors.
B. Update the schedule baseline to reflect the current team's velocity.
C. Arrange for specialized training for the team and update the resource management plan.
D. Request the project sponsor for more budget to hire a technical lead.

**Question 5**
Two departments are in a heated disagreement over the priority of features for a new internal tool. The conflict is threatening to delay the project's initiation phase. What should the project manager do to build shared understanding?
A. Use a Power/Interest grid to decide which department’s needs are more important.
B. Facilitate a workshop with key stakeholders from both departments to identify common goals and prioritize requirements.
C. Ask the project sponsor to make the final decision on feature priority.
D. Document both departments' requirements in the project scope statement and let the dev team decide what to build first.

**Question 6**
A project manager has recently inherited a team that is in the "Storming" stage of development. Team members are frequently challenging the project manager's authority and ignoring the ground rules. What is the MOST effective leadership style for the project manager at this stage?
A. Pacesetting: Set high standards and demonstrate excellence to inspire the team.
B. Delegating: Step back and allow the team to resolve their own internal conflicts.
C. Coaching: Focus on individual development and building collaborative relationships.
D. Autocratic: Assert authority and strictly enforce the ground rules to maintain order.

**Question 7**
A team member is consistently underperforming, causing delays in their assigned work packages. The project manager has already provided informal coaching, but there hasn't been an improvement. What should the project manager do NEXT?
A. Report the underperformance to the functional manager for a formal performance review.
B. Reassign the team member's tasks to a higher-performing member to protect the schedule.
C. Conduct a formal meeting with the team member to discuss the performance gap and develop a performance improvement plan.
D. Terminate the team member’s contract and find a replacement.

**Question 8**
During a project negotiation with a vendor, the project manager realizes that the vendor’s price is significantly higher than the project’s budget baseline. What should the project manager do?
A. Accept the vendor's price but reduce the project's scope to compensate for the cost.
B. Walk away from the negotiation and find a different vendor immediately.
C. Analyze the vendors' pricing structure and seek areas for collaborative cost reduction or alternative delivery methods.
D. Request the project sponsor for additional funding to meet the vendor's price.

**Question 9**
A project manager is working with a diverse team. One team member repeatedly makes culturally insensitive jokes during lunch breaks, making others uncomfortable. This behavior is not covered by the current team charter. What should the project manager do?
A. Ignore the behavior as it occurs during breaks and is not related to project work.
B. Terminate the team member immediately for violating organizational ethics.
C. Facilitate a team meeting to update the ground rules and define acceptable behavior in the team charter.
D. Speak to the team member privately and tell them to stop making jokes.

**Question 10**
An agile team is struggling with a high number of impediments that are outside of their control. The team is feeling demotivated. What is the project manager’s role in this situation?
A. Tell the team to focus on the work they can control and ignore the outside impediments.
B. Escalate all impediments to the project management office (PMO) for resolution.
C. Act as a servant leader to identify, prioritize, and actively work to remove the impediments for the team.
D. Reassign the team members to a different project where they won't face these blockers.

**Question 11**
A project manager is using emotional intelligence to lead a team through a high-stress phase. They notice a team member who is usually very active has become withdrawn and quiet. What is the FIRST thing the project manager should do?
A. Assign the team member more challenging tasks to snap them out of their mood.
B. Mention the behavior during the daily stand-up to encourage team support.
C. Observe the team member’s interactions and then have a private, empathetic conversation to understand their state.
D. Assume the team member is just tired and will recover after the weekend.

**Question 12**
A key stakeholder is resistant to a new project because they believe it will make their current role redundant. What is the BEST strategy for the project manager to manage this stakeholder?
A. Avoid communicating with the stakeholder as they are likely to be negative.
B. Provide the stakeholder with a copy of the project charter to prove the project's value.
C. Engage the stakeholder in a discussion to understand their fears and look for opportunities for them to contribute to the project's success.
D. Inform the stakeholder that the project is mandated by the CEO and they must support it.

**Question 13**
A project manager is facilitating a negotiation for a new contract. They want to ensure a "Win-Win" outcome for both the project and the vendor. What technique should they use?
A. Focus on hard bargaining and stick strictly to the project's initial offer.
B. Use a collaborative approach that focuses on shared interests and explores creative options for mutual gain.
C. Be prepared to walk away from the negotiation if the vendor does not agree to all terms immediately.
D. Give in to the vendor's demands to ensure the contract is signed on time.

**Question 14**
A team member asks the project manager for permission to work remotely two days a week. The project manager believes this would improve the member's work-life balance but is worried about the impact on team collaboration. What should the project manager do?
A. Deny the request to ensure the team stays together in the office.
B. Allow the request without consulting the team.
C. Discuss the request with the team and collaboratively develop a hybrid working agreement that maintains productivity and collaboration.
D. Tell the team member to ask their functional manager instead.

**Question 15**
A project manager is mentoring a junior developer on the team. The developer expresses interest in moving into a lead role in the future. What is the BEST mentoring action?
A. Tell the developer to wait until they have at least five more years of experience.
B. Provide the developer with opportunities to lead small tasks or modules and provide constructive feedback on their performance.
C. Send the developer to a one-week leadership training course and consider the mentoring complete.
D. Give the developer a list of leadership books to read on their own time.

**Question 16**
During a project, the project manager discovers that a requirement was misunderstood, leading to the development of a feature that doesn't meet the stakeholder's needs. What should the project manager do FIRST?
A. Scold the team member who developed the feature for not asking for clarification.
B. Conduct a root cause analysis to understand why the misunderstanding occurred.
C. Immediately submit a change request to fix the feature.
D. Delete the feature and move on to the next task in the backlog.

**Question 17**
A project manager is leading a team of experts from different departments. The team is struggling to agree on a common vocabulary for the project, leading to frequent confusion. What is the BEST action for the project manager?
A. Create a project glossary and mandate that everyone use the terms as defined.
B. Allow the team to continue using their own terminology and hope they eventually understand each other.
C. Facilitate a meeting where the team collaboratively defines key terms and establishes a shared project language.
D. Ask the most senior expert to define the terminology for the rest of the team.

**Question 18**
A project is falling behind schedule. The project manager notices that the team is spending a lot of time in unproductive meetings. What should the project manager do to remove this impediment?
A. Cancel all meetings and tell the team to communicate only via email.
B. Personally attend every meeting to ensure they stay on track.
C. Work with the team to establish clear agendas, timeboxes, and outcomes for all project meetings.
D. Hire a professional facilitator to run all project meetings.

**Question 19**
A project manager wants to ensure that all team members feel empowered to make decisions within their areas of expertise. What should the project manager do to support this?
A. Tell the team they are now empowered and should stop asking for permission.
B. Define clear boundaries and authority levels for decision-making in the project's governance framework.
C. Have every team member sign an "Empowerment Agreement" and file it in the project artifacts.
D. Personally review every decision made by the team to ensure it's correct.

**Question 20**
A stakeholder believes that a project risk has just materialized into an issue, but the project manager is not sure. What should the project manager do FIRST?
A. Immediately log the issue and begin working on a resolution.
B. Verify the evidence and analyze the situation to determine if a risk has indeed become an issue.
C. Tell the stakeholder they are overreacting and to wait and see what happens.
D. Update the risk register to show the risk is now higher priority.
