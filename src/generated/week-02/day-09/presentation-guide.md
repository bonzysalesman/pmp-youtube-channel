# Presentation Guide: Day 9 - Defining Operational Ground Rules

## 🎬 Overall Flow
- **Verbatim Hook**: "Good morning/afternoon, team. Welcome back to Day 9. Yesterday we curated our team’s talent. Today, we define our operational excellence. We are moving from 'who is on the team' to 'how we work together.' We’re going to facilitate the creation of a social contract—the Team Charter—that will serve as our primary governing document for the next 13 weeks.”
- **Learning Objectives**: Facilitate the co-creation of ground rules and understand their strategic role in de-escalating conflict within the People Domain.

## 📊 Slide-by-Slide Breakdown

### Slide 1: Title Slide
**Notes (Verbatim):**
"Welcome back. Today we address the 'rules of engagement.' For our professional network—this is about building a scalable culture. We’re deep-diving into Section 1.12 of the ECO: Defining Team Ground Rules.”

---

### Slide 2: The Strategic Value of Ground Rules
**Notes (Verbatim):**
"In a professional project environment, performance isn't left to chance. Ground rules provide the proactive framework that mitigates conflict before it compromises our velocity. They are the essential table stakes for high-performing teams.”

---

### Slide 3: Defining the "Social Contract"
**Notes (Verbatim):**
"Think of ground rules as a professional social contract. These aren't just 'do and don't' lists; they are the explicit agreements on how we communicate, how we make decisions, and how we disagree. This clarity is what allows a senior cohort to operate with agility.”

---

### Slide 4: Analytical Pillars of Team Governance
**Notes (Verbatim):**
"Ownership is the primary driver of adherence. As PMs, we don't 'give' the team rules; we facilitate their creation via consensus. This co-creation ensures buy-in and fosters the psychological safety necessary for complex delivery.”

---

### Slide 5: Alignment: Organizational Strategy (Task 1.12.1)
**Notes (Verbatim):**
"We must calibrate our team norms with the broader organizational context. We look at the corporate Code of Ethics and Communication Policies to ensure our Charter is a cohesive extension of the business environment.”

---

### Slide 6: Fostering Adherence & Accountability (Task 1.12.2)
**Notes (Verbatim):**
"We lead by example. If the Charter says meetings start at 08:00, we are there at 07:55. We also ensure these norms are visible—in our Slack headers, our Trello boards, or our project rooms. Visibility drives accountability.”

---

### Slide 7: Responding to Violations (Task 1.12.3)
**Notes (Verbatim):**
"Consistency is key. If a norm is violated, we address it promptly and privately. We focus on the 'System Gap'—the violation of the agreement—rather than a personal failing. This approach maintains professional respect while upholding our standards.”

---

### Slide 8: Core Categories for Professional Norms
**Notes (Verbatim):**
"We focus our agreements on four high-impact areas: Communication velocity, meeting rigor, collaborative resource sharing, and structured conflict resolution. This is the operational engine of our team.”

---

### Slide 9: The Team Charter: The Critical Asset
**Notes (Verbatim):**
"The Charter is our primary operational artifact. On the exam, when you encounter a recurring behavioral issue, your first action is often to 'Revisit the Team Charter.' It is our 'North Star' for internal consistency.”

---

### Slide 10: Conclusion: Operational Excellence
**Notes (Verbatim):**
"To conclude: ground rules are a fundamental requirement for team performance. By investing in this social contract early, we provide our team with the stability they need to tackle the technical challenges ahead.”

---

### Slide 11: Strategic Workshop: Drafting the Charter
**Notes (Verbatim):**
"Let’s apply this. In your groups, draft a high-impact set of ground rules for a distributed, multi-time-zone team. Focus on communication SLAs and decision-making models. Let's see how we design for performance.”

---

### Slide 12: Tactical Next Steps
**Notes (Verbatim):**
"Review Section 1.12 tonight. Tomorrow we move to Day 10: Negotiating Project Agreements. We’ll be looking at how we manage the boundaries between our team and external stakeholders.”
"Our norms are set. Now we move to negotiation. See you tomorrow."

## 🎯 Practice Application (Strategic Perspective)
- **Scenario**: A lead architect is frequently 10 minutes late to the Daily Standup and argues they are 'too busy for administrative rules.'
- **Insight**: "Your professional instinct might be a direct reprimand. However, the PMP Mindset points toward the **Social Contract**. 'As a team, we agreed in our Charter that the standup is a 15-minute high-velocity session that requires everyone present.' Frame the issue as a failure to uphold the team agreement, not a failure of their work. This maintains the relationship while enforcing the standard."
