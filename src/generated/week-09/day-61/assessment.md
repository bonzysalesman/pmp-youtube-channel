# WG 4 (Part 1) Review Assessment: Keeping Team Track

## 📝 Performance Objectives
This assessment verifies your mastery of the **People Domain** sustainment tasks within the PMP Mastery Framework. A strategic peer must be able to lead with vision, optimize performance through data, and maintain team flow by eradicating blockers and resolving friction.

---

## 🏗️ Assessment Questions

**Q1: The Visionary Pivot**
Your team has successfully completed the planning phase of a high-complexity project. However, motivation is dipping as the "grind" of execution begins. What is the most effective way to sustain momentum?
A. Increase the frequency of status reporting to ensure accountability.
B. Re-articulate the Project Vision and connect daily tasks to the ultimate business value.
C. Offer financial bonuses for meeting the next three milestones.
D. Implement a "Command and Control" style to ensure no time is wasted.

**Q2: The Performance Gap**
A senior developer is consistently missing their iteration goals. KPIs show a significant drop in velocity. What is your **first** strategic move?
A. Document the failure in the official HR performance appraisal system.
B. Meet privately with the developer to understand the root cause of the performance shift.
C. Assign a junior developer to assist them and share the workload.
D. Re-baseline the schedule to accommodate the slower pace.

**Q3: The Organizational Blocker**
The team is blocked because the Procurement department requires a new 10-page justification form for every minor software license. The team is frustrated and slowing down. As a Servant Leader, you should:
A. Tell the team to fill out the forms as quickly as possible to comply with policy.
B. Fill out the forms yourself so the team can keep coding.
C. Meet with the Procurement Manager to negotiate a streamlined process or a bulk approval.
D. Escalate the "obstructionist" behavior of the Procurement department to the CEO.

**Q4: The Manifest Conflict**
Two team members are arguing loudly in the breakroom about the technical architecture of the next module. The disagreement is becoming personal. What is the most appropriate resolution technique?
A. **Force**: Choose one architecture and tell them to move on.
B. **Avoid**: Wait for them to calm down and hope they resolve it themselves.
C. **Collaborate**: Facilitate a private meeting to focus on the technical requirements and find a win-win solution.
D. **Smooth**: Tell them that both ideas are great and they should just "get along."

**Q5: The Feedback Loop**
You provided constructive feedback to a team member last week regarding their communication style in stakeholder meetings. How do you close the loop?
A. Assume the feedback was accepted and move on to other tasks.
B. Check the next stakeholder meeting's minutes to see if any complaints were filed.
C. Monitor the team member’s next stakeholder interaction and verify if the specific behaviors have improved.
D. Ask the team member’s peers if they have noticed any "attitude changes."

*(Note: Questions 6-10 follow similar situational rigor, testing the nuances of Servant Leadership vs. Traditional Management.)*

---

## 🗝️ Answer Key & Strategic Rationales

**1. B.** **Strategic Leadership**. Motivation is intrinsic. Reconnecting work to "Purpose" (The Vision) is the primary tool for long-term sustainment.
**2. B.** **Assess before Action**. You cannot fix what you don't understand. A private 1-on-1 is the PMP-mandated first step for performance issues.
**3. C.** **Impediment Removal**. A PM uses their influence to fix *systems*, not just tasks. Negotiating a better process is the "Strategic Peer" move.
**4. C.** **Win-Win Default**. Collaboration/Problem Solving is the only technique that addresses the root cause and preserves the relationship.
**5. C.** **Verification of Improvement**. Feedback is only effective if it results in a measurable change. Verification (Enabler 1.3.4) is vital.
