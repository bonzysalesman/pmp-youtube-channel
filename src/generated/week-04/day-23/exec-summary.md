# Executive Summary: Day 23 - Plan & Manage Scope

## 🎯 Strategic Objective
In the **PMP Mastery Framework**, Day 23 focuses on **Scope Integrity**. As a strategic peer, you know that scope management is the art of protecting the project from the "death by a thousand cuts" (scope creep). Your goal is to ensure the project includes all the work required—and **only** the work required—to deliver the intended value.

---

## 🔑 Key Strategic Pillars (ECO 2.8)

### 1. Requirements Intelligence (Enabler 2.8.1)
Scope begins with the **Elicitation and Prioritization** of requirements. 
- **The Filter**: Not all requirements are equal. Use MoSCoW (Must, Should, Could, Won't) or Kano analysis to prioritize what drives the most value.
- **Agile Context**: Requirements are dynamic and captured in the **Product Backlog** as User Stories.

### 2. Hierarchical Decomposition (Enabler 2.8.2)
Breaking down the "Big Picture" into manageable units of work.
- **The WBS (Predictive)**: A deliverable-oriented hierarchical decomposition. The "Work Package" is the lowest level and the foundation for estimating and tracking.
- **The Backlog (Agile)**: An ordered list of value-items. Decomposition happens through Epics, Features, and Stories.
- **Scope Baseline**: Consists of the Project Scope Statement, WBS, and WBS Dictionary.

### 3. Scope Validation and Stewardship (Enabler 2.8.3)
Governing the boundaries of the project throughout its lifecycle.
- **Validation**: The formal process of getting stakeholder sign-off on completed deliverables. It’s about **Acceptance**, not just quality.
- **Control**: Monitoring status and managing changes. A strategic peer uses formal **Change Control** to prevent scope creep while remaining adaptable to high-value shifts.

---

## 💡 Strategic Mindset Pivot
> "Scope is not a static document; it is a clinical boundary. Every addition must prove its value against the project's objectives. Prevention of scope creep is a leadership discipline, not just a procedural check."
