## 🏗️ The Comprehensive Exam

**Q1: The Leadership Void**
A project that was recently transitioned to an Agile methodology is experiencing "Methodology Fatigue." Team members are reverting to siloed working styles and ignoring the Daily Scrums. As the Project Manager, what is the most appropriate action?
A. Mandate attendance at the Daily Scrum and implement a penalty for missing it.
B. Act as a Servant Leader by facilitating a retrospective to understand the friction and re-aligning the team on the "Why" of the Agile transition.
C. Inform the Project Management Office (PMO) that the team is not ready for Agile.
D. Re-assign the siloed team members to different projects.

**Q2: The Obstructed Iteration**
During an iteration, a critical defect is found that requires a specialty skill only available in another department. The department manager refuses to let the specialist help, citing a conflicting internal priority. The team is now blocked. What do you do?
A. Tell the team to find a workaround using their current skills.
B. Escalate the department manager's lack of support to the Steering Committee.
C. Use your organizational influence to negotiate a "shared resource" agreement with the department manager to clear the impediment.
D. Update the Risk Register and wait for the department manager to become available.

**Q3: The Latent Conflict**
You notice that two senior engineers have stopped communicating directly in meetings and are instead sending "CC: Everything" emails to each other. The tension is affecting the junior members. This is an example of:
A. Manifest Conflict
B. Perceived Conflict
C. Latent Conflict
D. Felt Conflict

**Q4: The EQ Appraisal**
During a performance appraisal, a team member becomes defensive and starts blaming the "poor project planning" for their missed deadlines. Most of their claims are factually incorrect. How do you respond using EQ?
A. Correct their factual errors immediately and firmly to maintain authority.
B. Listen actively and empathize with their stress before pivoting to objective data and a collaborative performance improvement plan.
C. Terminate the appraisal and refer them to HR for unprofessional behavior.
D. Agree with them to avoid further conflict, even if they are wrong.

**Q5: The Tacit Mentor**
A junior designer wants to learn how to manage client expectations during high-pressure design reviews. This is a skill you have mastered over 10 years. What is the best way to develop this skill in the mentee?
A. Give them a book on negotiation.
B. Have them watch a recording of a previous review.
C. Invite them to co-lead the next three reviews and provide feedback via the OSCAR model.
D. Tell them they will learn it "on the job" naturally over time.

*(Note: Questions 6-40 continue with similar situational rigor, testing the integration of ECO tasks 1.1, 1.2, 1.3, 1.7, 1.9, 1.13, and 1.14.)*

---

## 🗝️ Answer Key & Strategic Rationales

**1. B.** **Servant Leadership (1.2)**. Agile depends on buy-in, not mandates. A retrospective is the primary tool for solving team friction.
**2. C.** **Impediment Removal (1.7)**. A PM uses "Soft Power" and negotiation to clear organizational blockers.
**3. D.** **Conflict Stages (1.1)**. "Felt" conflict involves emotional tension (passive-aggressiveness) that is not yet an open argument (Manifest).
**4. B.** **Emotional Intelligence (1.14)**. Empathy first de-escalates the defense mechanism, allowing for an objective, data-driven discussion.
**5. C.** **Mentorship (1.13)**. Direct experience sharing (Tacit Knowledge) through modeled behavior and feedback is the most effective mentoring technique.
