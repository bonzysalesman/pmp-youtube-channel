# Presentation Guide: Day 65 - The Sage of the System

## 🗣️ Facilitator Persona: The Intellectual Capitalist
Today’s session is for the **Knowledge Stewards**. You are teaching your peers how to move beyond being "delivery drivers" to being "talent builders." Your tone should be **Thoughtful**, **Encouraging**, and **Philosophical**. You are the mentor to the mentors.

---

## 🧭 Slide-by-Slide Briefing

### Slide 1: Introduction
- **Hook**: "If the project ends and the team is the same as when it started, you’ve hit the target but missed the point. Welcome to Day 65."

### Slide 2: Objectives
- **Strategic Focus**: The transfer of **Experience** (Tacit Knowledge).

### Slide 3: Coaching vs. Mentoring
- **The Core Focus**: Distinction.
- **Peer Insight**: "Ask them: 'Who was a mentor to you?' That person likely didn't just teach you how to fill out a form; they taught you how to think. That's the goal today."

### Slide 4: The OSCAR Framework
- **The Technical "Why"**: The need for rigor in soft skills.
- **Coaching Tip**: "The PMP loves structured models. OSCAR provides a repeatable process for something that feels subjective."

### Slide 5: Tacit Knowledge
- **The Strategy**: Value recognition.
- **Peer Insight**: "Explicit knowledge is cheap; everyone has the PMBOK. Tacit knowledge is expensive; it only comes from time in the arena. Mentoring is how we keep that expensive value in the organization."

### Slide 6: Teachable Moments
- **The Strategy**: Proactive observation.
- **Coaching Note**: "A teachable moment is a short-term failure handled with long-term empathy. Don't let a good mistake go to waste."

---

## 🎯 Activity Debrief: "The Aspirational Leader"
**Objective**: Implementing long-term professional development.
- **Action**: **Mentorship & Influence**.
- **Resolution**: This is a **Mentoring** case. The junior PM's technical performance is fine (explicit), but their leadership perspective needs growth (tacit). Use the **OSCAR** model. Start by asking for their **Outcome**: "Where do you want to be in 2 years?" Then move to the **Situation**: "How is your current relationship with the senior stakeholders?"

---

## 🛑 Common Pitfalls to Address
- **"I don't have time to mentor"**: Correct this. Mentoring is a form of risk mitigation. A more capable team makes fewer mistakes.
- **Directing instead of Mentoring**: Warn them against giving the answer. A mentor provides the **framework** for the mentee to find their *own* answer.
- **Tacit vs. Explicit confusion**: Remind them: If you can write it in a checklist, it's Coaching/Training. If you have to tell a story about it, it's Mentoring.
