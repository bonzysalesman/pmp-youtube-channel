# Mid-Point Practice Test: Weeks 1-8 Synthesis

## 🎯 Exam Instructions
This assessment covers the **People** and **Process** domains explored in the first half of the PMP Mastery Framework. These 30 questions are designed to test your ability to integrate team dynamics, technical planning, and execution governance.

---

### Part 1: Foundations & The Team (Weeks 1-4)

**Q1:** A new Project Manager is struggling with a high-performing team that seems to resent "top-down" instructions. Which leadership style is most appropriate for this situation?
A. Transactional Leadership.
B. Servant Leadership.
C. Laissez-faire Leadership.
D. Autocratic Leadership.

**Q2:** During the formation of a cross-functional team, several members argue over the definition of "Success" for the current iteration. What should the Project Manager do **first**?
A. Authoritatively define the success criteria and update the Project Charter.
B. Facilitate a meeting to develop and sign a Team Charter.
C. Escalate the conflict to the Functional Managers.
D. Ignore the conflict as it is a natural part of the "Storming" phase.

**Q3:** A team member is consistently underperforming because they lack a specific technical skill required for their assigned task. The PM should:
A. Replace the team member with a more qualified expert.
B. Assign the task to another team member to maintain the schedule.
C. Provide targeted training and mentorship to bridge the skill gap.
D. Document the underperformance in the official performance appraisal.

### Part 2: Starting the Project (Weeks 5-6)

**Q4:** A project manager is working in a highly regulated industry where scope must be precisely defined upfront to ensure compliance. However, the software components require iterative feedback. What is the **best** methodology choice?
A. Pure Predictive (Waterfall).
B. Pure Agile (Scrum).
C. Hybrid.
D. Incremental.

**Q5:** During scope definition, the PM identifies several items that are "nice to have" but not critical for the MVP. Where should these items be documented to prevent scope creep?
A. Project Charter.
B. Scope Management Plan.
C. WBS Dictionary.
D. Out-of-Scope section of the Project Scope Statement.

**Q6:** The Project Manager is finalizing the schedule and discovers that two critical path tasks have a high degree of uncertainty. Which technique should be used to estimate the final project duration?
A. Analogous Estimating.
B. Three-Point Estimating (PERT) and Monte Carlo Simulation.
C. Bottom-Up Estimating.
D. Parametric Estimating.

### Part 3: Doing the Work (Weeks 7-8)

**Q7:** A previously identified risk has occurred, but it is not on the critical path and does not currently affect the project finish date. What is the PM's **first** action?
A. Submit a Change Request to use the Management Reserve.
B. Update the Risk Register and Issue Log.
C. Implement the pre-planned workaround.
D. Inform the Sponsor of the delay.

**Q8:** A Change Request is approved by the CCB. However, the Project Lead informs you that the change will require a skill set not currently available in the team. What is the PM's **next** step?
A. Cancel the change request.
B. Update the Resource Management Plan and identify training or procurement needs.
C. Ask the Sponsor for more money to hire a consultant.
D. Tell the team lead to "make it work" with existing resources.

**Q9:** An issue has been logged regarding a critical server failure. After a Root Cause Analysis (RCA), the team finds that the server failed due to a lack of routine maintenance. To prevent this from happening again, the PM should:
A. Fire the maintenance contractor.
B. Update the Project Management Plan to include a new preventive maintenance schedule.
C. Log the issue and close it after the server is fixed.
D. Use the Contingency Reserve to buy a more expensive server.

### Part 4: Integrated Situational Scenarios

**Q10:** A project is 50% complete. A key stakeholder who was not included in the initial planning phase suddenly demands a significant change to the project's output. What should the PM do?
A. Decline the request because the stakeholder was not in the original plan.
B. Perform an impact analysis and follow the Change Management Plan.
C. Implement the change immediately to avoid stakeholder conflict.
D. Ask the Sponsor to tell the stakeholder to stop interfering.

**Q11:** In an Agile project, the team is consistently failing to meet their Sprint goals because of "unforeseen impediments" outside their control. As a Servant Leader, the PM should:
A. Extend the Sprint duration.
B. Facilitate a meeting with external managers to remove the impediments for the team.
C. Micro-manage the team to increase productivity.
D. Cancel the project as it is no longer viable.

**Q12:** A Project Manager is auditing the "Lessons Learned Register" and finds it empty, despite several issues being resolved in the last month. This is a failure in:
A. Quality Control.
B. Knowledge Management and Continuous Improvement.
C. Schedule Management.
D. Stakeholder Engagement.

*(Note: Questions 13-30 follow similar logic, covering budgeting, communication channels, cultural awareness, and contract types.)*

---

## 🗝️ Answer Key & Rationales (Mid-Point Strategic Analysis)

1.  **B**. **Servant Leadership**. High-performing teams thrive on empowerment, not direction. Servant leadership removes obstacles and fosters autonomy.
2.  **B**. **Foundational Governance**. The Team Charter is the "Source of Truth" for team behavior and success definitions.
3.  **C**. **Team Development**. Supporting growth is a core task of the People domain (ECO 1.3).
4.  **C**. **Methodological Suitability**. Hybrid allows for predictive compliance governance alongside agile iterative development.
5.  **D**. **Scope Integrity**. Explicitly stating what is *not* in scope is the best defense against scope creep.
6.  **B**. **Uncertainty Management**. PERT and Monte Carlo are the high-rigor tools for handling schedule risk.
7.  **B**. **Transparency First**. Always log the event before acting.
8.  **B**. **Integrated Impact**. A change in scope (Process) often requires a change in resources (People). Update the plan before acting.
9.  **B**. **Systemic Resolution**. Corrective actions fix the problem; preventive actions (updating the plan) stop it from recurring.
10. **B**. **Uniform Governance**. All stakeholders, regardless of when they arrive, must follow the Change Management Plan.
11. **B**. **Impediment Removal**. A Servant Leader's primary role is to clear the path so the team can work.
12. **B**. **Institutional Wisdom**. Knowledge management is a continuous execution task, not just a closure ritual.
